      Keeping this private.

      If someone might check your phone, laptop or accounts, a few small habits keep this between you and the people helping you.

      Use a private window

      Private browsing doesn't keep history on the device. In Chrome it's New Incognito window. On an iPhone in Safari, tap the tabs button and choose Private. On Android, open Chrome's menu and choose New Incognito tab.

      It won't hide what you do from a work or school network, or from spyware on the device.

      Clear just this site

      You don't need to wipe everything, which can look unusual. Open your browser history, search for The Quiet Path and delete only those entries.

      This site also saves your notes on this device. .

      Shared devices

      On a shared computer or tablet, use your own login or a private window. Check what's open before you hand a phone over, and turn off message previews on your lock screen.

      Synced accounts

      If you share a Google, Apple or Microsoft account, your history, photos, calendar and saved passwords may show up on his devices too. Use an email and calendar that are only yours, and change any passwords he might know.

      The Quick exit button in the corner takes you straight to a weather page. Pressing Esc twice does the same.

      For more on staying safe, see Women's Refuge NZ: help and online safety ↗. If you're ever in danger, call 111.

      Online and data privacy.

      He may still know your logins, or be able to guess them. Lock every account down before anything else changes.

      Change every password

      Every site and every account. Start with your email, because every other password can be reset through it. Then do your banks, credit cards, finance company accounts, phone, power, internet, council, myIR, MyMSD, KiwiSaver, Apple or Google, and social media.

      Use a different password for each one, and nothing he could guess.

      Add a passcode wherever you can

      Banks, phone companies, power companies and councils often let you set a security passcode or PIN that has to be given before anyone can talk about or change your account.

      If you can't see the option, ring them and ask whether they offer one. Turn on two-step login with codes sent to your own phone.

      Before you tell the bank

      Don't close a joint account or tell the bank you've separated until you know what it does to your loan or mortgage. Banks act when they see a risk.

      If the bank thinks the mortgage can't be paid, because he's living in the house, you're paying rent somewhere else and he isn't paying child support, it can start steps to protect itself, and that can end with the house being sold. Talk to your family lawyer first.

      Your own account

      Have your pay, Working for Families and child support go into an account in your name only, with its own login and passcode. Keep the statements in your own email, not one he can see.
